CVFR Nav — Privacy Policy

Effective: 8 October 2026

CVFR Nav is a moving-map assistant for VFR pilots in Israel. This policy covers the version of the app on the App Store.

What the app collects

The developer receives no data from the App Store version of CVFR Nav: no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking. (The developer's own services are hosted by Vercel, which keeps short-lived request logs — see Services the app contacts.) The one thing that leaves the device on its own is an approximate position for the weather forecast, sent to Open-Meteo and not linked to you (see Weather below).

What stays on your device

  • Your position, from the device's location services, is used on the device to draw the map and raise alerts. It is not sent to the developer.
  • Recorded flights (track, altitude, the device's motion and barometer readings, events, a line about the device's health once a minute — the app's processor use, battery level, how warm the device is, whether Low Power Mode is on — and what the map showed during the flight: the active flight plan's route and aircraft — callsign, type, persons on board, not your filed plan's text with your name, licence and phone, and not the weight-and-balance load (the weights of pilot and passengers), which stays with the saved plan on the device — the NOTAMs in force, the radio reports and the aerodrome weather reports) and, if you switch it on, cockpit audio are saved in the app's own storage on your device. They leave it when you share or export a flight yourself, to the destination you choose, and — with Sync flights with iCloud on (Settings → Recording and audio; on unless you switch it off) — in your own iCloud Drive, so your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account have the same flights — and the flight videos you make, which leave the device a week after they reached iCloud and stay there until you delete them. That copy is stored by Apple under your account (Apple's privacy policy); the developer cannot read it. It is also shown in the Files app (iCloud Drive → CVFR Nav). Each flight goes with its signature, made with a key that never leaves this device, and that key's public half — the same for every flight from the device — so your other devices can tell when a flight was changed after it was signed. Deleting a flight in the app deletes it on all your devices. CVFR Nav assumes your Apple Account is yours alone and that every device signed in to it is your own: an account or a device shared between several people (a club iPad, say) shares their flights and cockpit audio too, at the account holder's own choice and responsibility — switch sync off on such a device.
  • Flight videos you make are kept on the device until you delete them (or, with sync on, a week after they reached iCloud, as above). Save to Photos adds a video to your photo library when you tap it; the app asks only to add photos, never to read them.
  • Flight briefs (PDF) hold the plan with your name, licence and phone. The app keeps them only until you have shared or saved them; they are cleared at the next launch.
  • Weather: for the forecast, the app sends Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) points rounded to about a kilometre — along a flight plan's route, the aircraft's position for the wind shown on the map (again only after 10 NM or 30 minutes), a spot you tap — with the hours wanted; nothing else about you. Open-Meteo keeps its request logs (which include those points and your internet address) for up to 90 days to run its service, and shares them with no one (its terms). The aerodrome reports come from the Israel Meteorological Service (ims.gov.il) and NOAA's Aviation Weather Center (aviationweather.gov) and need nothing from you. Nothing goes to the developer.
  • The barometer offset the app learns for your phone is kept on the device.
  • Photos of the sky for the cloud-cover estimate are analysed on the device and not stored or sent anywhere.
  • Settings and saved flight plans are stored on the device (a recording keeps a copy of the plan it was flown with, as above).

Services the app contacts

  • NOTAM feed — the app downloads the public NOTAM list from arielf-idra.github.io (GitHub Pages). The request carries no information about you or your position; like any web request it reaches GitHub with your device's IP address (GitHub's privacy statement).
  • Edition file — the app reads which edition of the CAA chart is currently published from arielf-idra.github.io/cvfr-nav/edition.json (GitHub Pages), to tell you when its data is out of date. The request carries nothing about you or your position.
  • Radio reports — while the app is open or recording a flight, it asks once a minute for the runway, wind and QNH read from public air traffic radio, from the developer's isr-atc service (llhz-now.vercel.app and isr-atc.idra-tech.io, hosted by Vercel). The request names only the airfield or unit; it carries nothing about you or your position. Like any web request it reaches the host with your device's IP address; Vercel keeps short-lived request logs (IP address, time, path), which the developer does not use to identify anyone (Vercel's privacy policy).
  • Aerodrome weather — every 10 minutes while the app is open or recording, the app reads the aerodrome reports and forecasts from the Israel Meteorological Service (ims.gov.il) and NOAA's Aviation Weather Center (aviationweather.gov). The requests carry nothing about you or your position; like any web request they reach those hosts with your device's IP address.
  • CVFR Nav's weather service — every 10 minutes while the app is open or recording, the app asks the developer's own service (on Vercel) for the Israel Meteorological Service's station readings. The request carries the app's key and nothing about you or your position; like any web request it reaches the host with your device's IP address. The developer's hosting provider, Vercel, keeps short-lived request logs (IP address, time, path), which the developer does not use to identify anyone (Vercel's privacy policy).
  • Apple Maps — the map background and the place name shown when you tap the map come from Apple's map services, under Apple's privacy policy.

Test builds (TestFlight)

Pre-release builds given to invited test pilots send recorded flights to the developer while "Send recordings to the developer" is on in Settings — on by default in those builds, and the tester can switch it off: the track, the sensor log, the settings the flight was recorded with, the plan flown (route, callsign, aircraft, persons on board — not the filed plan's text nor the weight-and-balance load), the device-health lines (processor use, battery, how warm, Low Power Mode), the NOTAMs and radio reports seen during the flight, crash reports, the name the tester enters, the flight's signature with this device's public signing key (the same on every flight from the device, so it identifies the device; the developer's check notes a signing key not seen before for that tester), and the cockpit audio only if its own switch (off by default) is on too. The App Store version cannot send anything. They are stored in Apple's CloudKit for the developer to find problems, and deleted on request.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone.
